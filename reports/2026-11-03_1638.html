Tax case report: Ismail bin Idris v. DGIR (High Court Penang, 23/10/2026) – gain on 150 land lots held 11 years taxed as business income, not RPGT

  • Section: Legislation (everything)
  • Publication date: 23/10/2026 (decision); added to HASiL Tax Case page 03/11/2026
  • Type: new
  • What changed: The taxpayer bought 150 converted building lots in Kedah for RM850,000 in 2004 from a company he was a director of, and sold them all to PR1MA in 2015 for RM44 million, reporting the sale under RPGT (Form CKHT 1A). HASiL raised an additional assessment treating the gain as business income under s.4(a) ITA 1967, with a 45% penalty under s.113(2) (total RM15.64m), citing the badges of trade: the quantity and location of the land, the price gap, his construction and property experience, and no income earned during the holding period. The High Court dismissed his appeal with RM12,000 costs; it agreed that an 11-year holding period does not by itself prove an investment, and that filing an RPGT return (with no RPGT assessment raised) does not stop HASiL taxing the gain as income.
  • Link: https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20261023-REVENEWS-ISMAIL-BIN-IDRIS.pdf

Tax case report: DJM & 4 others v. DGIR (SCIT, 23/10/2026) – full RPGT exemption under P.U.(A) 218/2020 not limited to the land area under the house

  • Section: Legislation (everything)
  • Publication date: 23/10/2026 (decision); added to HASiL Tax Case page 03/11/2026
  • Type: new
  • What changed: Five co-owners who inherited an Ipoh residential property (about 111,073 sq ft) sold it in 2021 for RM5.35 million and claimed the full exemption under the RPGT (Exemption) Order 2020 [P.U.(A) 218/2020]. HASiL limited the exemption to a reasonable area around the house based on an internal technical circular, because the house's actual size wasn't provided. The Special Commissioners allowed the taxpayers' appeal and set aside the RPGT assessments (Form K) for YA 2021; the taxpayers had argued the Order sets no area limit and an internal circular cannot add conditions. HASiL may appeal within 21 days.
  • Link: https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20261023-REVENEWS-DJM-4-LAIN.pdf

News: Government to require foreign online commerce platforms (e.g. Temu, Pinduoduo) to adopt e-Invoice

  • Section: Pautan Popular > E-Invois
  • Publication date: 28/10/2026
  • Type: new
  • What changed: Linked from HASiL's e-Invoice news page (The Edge). Winding up the Budget 2027 debate, the Domestic Trade Minister said MOF will impose e-Invoice requirements on foreign online commerce platforms and their related vendors, which will also need to set up offices in Malaysia. These platforms already pay the 10% low-value goods tax, and a bill to regulate foreign platforms is planned for the first parliamentary sitting of 2027. This is an announcement; no implementation date or legal instrument has been published yet.
